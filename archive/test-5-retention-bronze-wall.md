# Test run — eval 5: retention-bronze-wall (iteration 1, skill v4)

## Active Role

Creative Director. (Motivation, progression, long-term goals.)

## Objective

Players clear Bronze 5 and quit. Diagnose why, then restore retention using only
MVP systems — no daily missions, login rewards, gacha, PvP, multiplayer, guilds,
or battle pass.

## Why players leave (the diagnosis before the medicine)

Finishing Bronze breaks two pillars at once. **Pillar 4 dies:** "next battle" was
the only visible goal, and it just ran out — nothing on screen says *Silver is
coming, here's what it asks of you*. **Pillar 1 stalls:** one leveled monster (or
three) beat Bronze, and the game never gave a reason to raise a second team or a
better individual. The player didn't get bored of the game; the game stopped
asking anything of them. Live-service tricks (missions, login streaks) paper over
this with appointments; the MVP fix is to make the *existing* loop pose a new
demand.

## Design Decision — three levers, all inside current systems

**1. Make league promotion a raising goal, not a queue.** Silver entry has a
visible requirement the player can see from Bronze 3 onward — e.g., "field a
team of three monsters at level 8+" or a team-power gate. Now clearing Bronze
isn't an ending; it's the moment the game says *go raise someone new / push your
Wolf further*. The loop's "unlock new challenge" step gets teeth, and roster
depth (Pillar 1 + 3) becomes the progression currency. Cost: one gate value in
`balance.json` + one lock-screen label.

**2. Long training as the organic return hook.** Add longer session tiers to the
existing timers — 2 h, 8 h "overnight camp" — with better (not strictly
efficient-per-hour) yields. The player ends tonight's session by *choosing
tomorrow's reason to return*: "Turtle's endurance camp finishes in the morning."
That is daily-return behavior generated by the pet-raising fantasy itself — an
appointment the player made with their monster, not a mission chart. Cost: rows
in the training config.

**3. League mastery grades.** Each cleared league shows a grade with two replay
goals per rung: *flawless* (no monster faints) and *swift* (under 45 s). Both are
computed from data the battle result already contains. They convert cleared
content into build-experimentation content (Pillar 3): a stall team that beat
Bronze won't take *swift*; a glass team won't take *flawless*. Cost: two checks +
a badge on the league screen.

## Expected retention impact

(1) attacks the quit point directly — the Bronze-5 wall becomes a visible ramp,
so session N ends with a concrete next objective (D1–D7 driver). (2) creates the
daily return rhythm the CD philosophy asks for, without a missions system
(D1–D30 driver). (3) multiplies content: five leagues × two grades ≈ ten
medium-term goals from zero new battles (D7–D30 driver). Directional claims —
validate with a funnel marker at Bronze 5 → Silver 1 after launch.

## Scope impact

All three are MVP Safe: config values, two result checks, and three UI labels.
No new systems, screens, currencies, or assets beyond one badge icon (available
in every approved UI pack). Self-check passed: nothing here delays Build Phases
1–3; item (3) can even slip to a fast-follow patch without harming (1) and (2).

## Scope Classification

MVP Safe.

## Recommendation

Implement (1) and (2) in the MVP — they repair the broken pillars at the exact
moment of churn. Hold (3) as the first post-launch patch if Build Phase 5 gets
tight. Record the Silver gate value and long-session yields in `balance.json`,
and the promotion-gate design in `game-spec.md`.
